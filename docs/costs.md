# Monthly operating costs

Generated by `scripts/cost_model.py` from measured sizes (`docs/evidence/stage9/performance.json`, commit `7109358f15b9`) and official prices copied on 2026-10-21 (`docs/evidence/stage9/prices.json`, sources listed at the end). This is an estimate, not a quote: prices change, taxes are excluded, and the workload below is an assumption to replace with real usage.

## Deployment priced

The topology in `render.yaml`: one Render web service on plan `1c-2g` running the job worker in-process, one Render PostgreSQL database on plan `0.1c-256mb`, and an S3-compatible bucket (Cloudflare R2 Standard) holding every original and artifact. Render's free plans are not used: a free web service spins down and loses local files, and a free database expires after 30 days. The workspace is on the `hobby` plan (one person; the Pro plan adds $25.00 per month for teams).

## Workload assumptions

- New measurements per month: 100, 1,000, 10,000; 1 default analysis each (runs A and B); everything kept; nothing deleted.
- Recording size profiles, measured on synthetic recordings submitted as gzip CSV:
  - `medium`: 1 h at 100 Hz (typical HVSR survey point)
  - `large`: 2 h at 200 Hz
  - `eight_hours`: 8 h at 50 Hz (largest the render.yaml limits admit)
- Object writes (R2 Class A): 60 per measurement. A real submission and its default analysis wrote 39 objects locally (identical bytes are stored once); S3 staging adds copies, so this is rounded up.
- Page views: 10 per stored measurement per month, each causing 10 bucket reads (R2 Class B) and 200 kB of HTML/JSON from Render. Figures and downloads are redirected to presigned bucket URLs, so their bytes leave the bucket, not Render.
- Downloads: 1 times everything stored, every month (deliberately high; only matters for B2 and Hetzner, R2 egress is free).
- Health checks: one `/readyz` per 5 s (518,400 bucket reads per month).
- Render outbound bandwidth: every stored byte is uploaded once from Render to the bucket (uploads to storage outside Render are billed as outbound traffic), plus page responses.
- PostgreSQL disk: measured database growth per measurement plus 10 MB, kept under 90% full on a 1 GB or multiple-of-5 GB disk.

## Capacity check

- 100 measurements per month of the largest profile need about 0.7 CPU hours (0.1% of one instance-month) at one job at a time.
- 1,000 measurements per month of the largest profile need about 7.4 CPU hours (1.0% of one instance-month) at one job at a time.
- 10,000 measurements per month of the largest profile need about 74.2 CPU hours (10.3% of one instance-month) at one job at a time.
- Bursts queue: jobs run one at a time (`HVSR_MAX_CONCURRENT_JOBS=1`), uploads are parsed one at a time per process, and further uploads wait up to `HVSR_UPLOAD_WAIT_S` and then get 503 busy.
- The 10,000-per-month rows keep the `0.1c-256mb` database; its 256 MB of RAM was not load-tested. Moving to `0.5c-1g` adds $13.00 per month.

## Not included

- Taxes, a custom domain, monitoring, and the Render Pro workspace for teams.
- An off-site copy of the bucket and database backups (recommended, see `docs/backup-restore.md`): at B2 prices about the B2 column again.
- Reanalyses and cosmetic figure variants: each extra analysis adds the artifact bytes again; figure variants are capped per run (`HVSR_MAX_PLOT_VARIANTS_PER_RUN`).
- Render's point-in-time recovery for paid databases is part of the database plan; its window depends on the workspace plan (see Render's documentation). It was not exercised here.
